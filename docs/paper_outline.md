# Paper Architecture

## Working Title

From Dish Label to Platform Commodity: Taiwanese Lu Rou Fan and the Consumption of Taiwaneseness in Mainland China

## Central Claim

In mainland China, "Taiwanese braised pork rice" does not simply reproduce a Taiwanese dish. Rather, it transforms Taiwaneseness into a consumable cultural sign through menu language, visual branding, platform representation, and consumer interpretation.

## Suggested Article Structure

### 1. Introduction

Open with the puzzle: why does a common rice bowl become marketable as "Taiwanese" in mainland urban food platforms? Introduce lu rou fan as a dish whose meaning is not fixed by recipe but mediated through naming, script, brand stories, platform tags, and consumer review discourse.

End the introduction with three research questions:

1. How does the meaning of lu rou fan / Taiwanese cuisine change from 1940 to 2020?
2. How does Taiwanese lu rou fan diffuse across mainland cities and regions?
3. Does this diffusion concentrate first in Shanghai/Yangtze River Delta circuits, broader coastal corridors, or inland fast-casual expansion?
4. Is the diffusion driven by Taiwan capital, Taiwan-mainland joint ventures, Hong Kong intermediary branding, mainland Taiwan-themed operators, or mall/platform wanghong economics?
5. How do Taiwan markers become attached to lu rou fan markers in mainland commercial discourse?
6. How do merchants stage Taiwaneseness through naming, menu language, visual branding, and platform representation?
7. How do consumers reproduce, transform, ignore, or reject this staged Taiwaneseness?

### 2. Literature Review

Organize the literature into four clusters:

1. Taiwanese cuisine, national cuisine, and identity.
2. Lu rou fan, orthography, memory, and cultural construction.
3. Authenticity, transnational food branding, and symbolic place-making.
4. Platform reviews, commercial signs, and computational cultural analysis.

The review should not simply list sources. Its job is to show that existing work explains food identity and authenticity, but has not yet measured how a specific Taiwanese dish label becomes a platformized cultural sign in mainland China.

### 3. Methodology

Present the study as a mixed-method design:

- spatial-temporal diffusion layer
- capital and ownership layer
- historical-semantic layer
- merchant-staging layer
- consumer-interpretation layer

Introduce three original instruments:

- YRD Concentration Index / Inland Penetration Index
- Capital-Origin Classification
- Taiwan-Lu Rou Fan Binding Index
- Taiwaneseness Lexicon Score
- Consumer Reinterpretation Gap

Emphasize transparency: source logging, original-text preservation, normalized text fields, manual validation, and ambiguity notes.

### 4. Findings I: Geo-Historical Sequence

Show the progression from broad Taiwanese dining to lu rou fan specialization:

- 1940-1945: late colonial food and market order; lu rou fan terminology requires archive verification rather than retrospective assumption.
- 1946-1949: postwar transition; Taiwan-side food hierarchy is reorganized after KMT takeover.
- 1950-1978: lu rou fan develops primarily as Taiwan-side stall/market popular food, with little mainland commercial circulation.
- 1979-1987: reform-era reopening creates background conditions but not yet direct platformized Taiwan-food commerce.
- 1988-2001: cross-strait mobility and investment make Taiwan food more legible.
- 2002-2013: Taiwan-linked casual dining enters through Shanghai/YRD as modern chain/leisure cuisine.
- 2014-2020: lu rou fan becomes bound to night-market and authenticity narratives through Hong Kong/Taiwan-linked origin stories.
- 2021-2023: Shanghai small-store and platform transition cases narrow Taiwaneseness into rice-bowl/snack formats.
- 2024-2025: lu rou fan specialty brands combine authenticity rhetoric, value pricing, prepared-food tension, mall/platform expansion, and wanghong visibility.

This section should establish the paper's main claim: the geography of diffusion and the history of semantic change are linked.

### 5. Findings II: Spatial Diffusion

Analyze whether the category is concentrated in Shanghai/Yangtze River Delta, distributed across coastal regions, or penetrating inland through mall/platform expansion. Keep open stores and planned stores separate.

This section should test:

- Yangtze River Delta Gateway Hypothesis
- Coastal-Corridor Hypothesis
- Inland Platformization Hypothesis

### 6. Findings III: Binding Taiwan to Lu Rou Fan

Show how Taiwan markers and dish markers co-occur across sources, time periods, cities, or platforms. Use descriptive statistics first. Add collocation or log-odds only if the data supports it.

### 7. Findings IV: Merchant Staging of Taiwaneseness

Compare merchant-side domains:

- geographic Taiwaneseness
- nostalgic Taiwaneseness
- night-market Taiwaneseness
- authenticity Taiwaneseness
- platform fast-food Taiwaneseness

This section should include examples from names, menu language, visual cues, and platform tags.

### 8. Findings V: Consumer Reinterpretation

Compare merchant scores with review scores. Use the Consumer Reinterpretation Gap to show where consumers accept, transform, ignore, reject, or compare Taiwaneseness.

The most important finding may be a gap rather than alignment: merchants may overproduce authenticity and geographic origin, while consumers may discuss taste, portion, value, convenience, nostalgia, or disappointment.

### 9. Discussion

Return to the theoretical point: Taiwaneseness operates not as a stable essence but as a movable commercial sign with a geography. In platform commerce, it can be condensed into authenticity claims, nostalgic atmosphere, night-market imagery, or fast-food value; as it diffuses spatially, these meanings may shift.

Keep causal claims modest. Search attention, platform visibility, and franchise expansion can be interpreted as contextual associations, not proof of political or cultural causation.

### 10. Conclusion

State the contribution clearly:

1. Empirical: a new spatial and platform case study of Taiwanese lu rou fan in mainland China.
2. Theoretical: a model of Taiwaneseness as a consumable cultural sign.
3. Methodological: a reproducible mixed-method design for studying food, place labels, and platform discourse.

## First Paragraph to Draft

The first paragraph should not begin with theory. Begin with the commercial object:

> On mainland Chinese food platforms, "Taiwanese braised pork rice" appears as a menu item, a shop category, a franchise format, and a searchable consumer object. Its Taiwaneseness is not carried by the dish alone. It is produced through names, scripts, images, origin stories, platform tags, and consumer reviews. This paper asks how a rice bowl becomes a cultural sign: how Taiwan is attached to lu rou fan, how merchants stage that attachment, and how consumers reinterpret it in everyday platform discourse.
